\begin{abstract}
Replacing the consensus engine of a running permissioned blockchain changes who can finalize
blocks and under which quorum rule. We present \sage{} (Shadow-Anchored Graceful Evolution),
a certified authority-boundary design for deterministic-finality engines sharing one fixed,
identical committee and monolithic state. The source remains the sole finalizer during shadow
execution; a deliberate certification pause precedes target activation. For arbitrary-subset
boundary certificates with $n$ validators and at most $f$ Byzantine, correct-signer intersection
and availability require $q\in((n+f)/2,n-f]$. In this certificate model, $2f+1$ does not ensure
intersection when $n>3f+1$; the result is not a claim about every BFT protocol. Under a durable
attestation lock and $q_1>2f$, an $n-f$ \CutCert{} already excludes further source finalization.
The \FenceCert{} instead supplies manifest- and policy-bound retirement evidence; it is not an
independent source-exclusion necessity in this locked design. A separate fence-only contract
requires $q_F+q_1>n+f$ and is available exactly when $q_1>2f$. These conditions apply known
quorum-intersection arguments to a migration-specific authority transfer. \ManCert{} authenticates
the migration body, while \AbortCert{} and \SealCert{} specify a bounded reversible window.
Bounded TLA+/TLC models with falsifying controls check finite instances but do not establish
implementation refinement. Concurrent-process controls on a partial PoA-to-HotStuff path with a
crash-only source observe conflicts at an unsafe threshold ($5/5$ trials) and none at a safe one
($0/5$); at a shared safe threshold, the \CutCert{} gate refuses $10$ uncertified crossings
accepted by an ungated control. These constructed controls do not execute the complete
committee-wide certificate path or measure its end-to-end cost.
\end{abstract}
